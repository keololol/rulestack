    How it works
    Rules in, ranking out.
    
      
    
        
    1. Yes or noEach rule from a law, treaty or watchdog scores 1 or 0.
    
        
    2. Topic scoreRules passed ÷ rules that apply.
    
        
    3. MatchTopic scores averaged with your bubble sizes.
    
        
    4. Your barsA level 3 bubble needs 3/5 of that topic passed.
    
        
    5. Your mixHighest return guide among what clears, spread by sector.
    
        
    6. Practice orderSolvers fine-tune it. Nothing is bought.
    
      
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Return and risk
    
        
      
          
    • Stocks: past 10-year average of the matching S&P 500 sector, 2016–2025 (Novel Investor). A sector proxy, not the company’s own return.
      • 
          
    • Government bonds: 10-year yield on 3–4 Nov 2026 (Trading Economics). Local currency, so currency swings are not included. Saudi Arabia has no figure.
      • 
          
    • Company green bonds: home government yield plus 1 point, our assumption.
      • 
          
    • Funds: 5-year return per year in euros (justETF, read Nov 2026). Green bond fund: yield after fees (Franklin factsheet, May 2026).
      • 
          
    • Risk 1–7: volatility mapped onto the EU PRIIPs bands. Developed-market bonds use the green bond fund’s 6.6%; emerging-market bonds use 14%, our assumption.
      • 
          
    • Good and bad year: return guide ± 1.28 × volatility, a rough 1-in-10 range.
      • 
        
    
        
    All of these look backward or use today’s yields. None of them is a forecast.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    The three solvers
    
        
      
          
    • LP: the highest return guide your rules allow. Simplex, written for this page, with shadow prices.
      • 
          
    • Branch and bound: the best order with few lines and a minimum ticket. It keeps or drops one name at a time.
      • 
          
    • NLP: return minus a penalty for volatility, λ/2 × variance. λ is 8 for Calm, 4 for Balanced, 2 for Bold. Frank–Wolfe solves it as a chain of LPs.
      • 
          
    • Shared limits: each sleeve within 10 points of your risk level, caps per holding and sector, and your bars.
      • 
          
    • Assumed: correlations of .6 between bonds, .65 between stocks and .15 across. Real ones move.
      • 
        
    
        
    Solvers work on past figures and today’s yields. A best answer here is best for this model, not for the future.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Why it’s built this way
    
        
      
          
    • Greenwashing and data trust are the top barriers, named by 70% of investors, while 99% of Gen Z say they are interested (Morgan Stanley, 2025). So every check links to its source.
      • 
          
    • ESG ratings from major providers correlate 0.54 on average, against 0.92 for credit ratings (Berg, Kölbel & Rigobon, 2022). Published yes/no rules can be checked by anyone.
      • 
          
    • Points and prize draws raised trading by 12% and pushed more trades into risky products in an FCA experiment. So progress here rewards learning and never rewards trading.
      • 
        
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Limits
    
        
      
          
    • Sample of 29 companies, 19 governments, 9 labelled bonds and 5 funds, checked by hand 29 Oct–4 Nov 2026. Lists change and errors are possible.
      • 
          
    • A high match means no rule here catches a company. It doesn’t prove the company is clean, and nothing here tests things like data-centre water use.
      • 
          
    • Some cut-offs are ours: a CPI score of 60, a 10-year bribery window, the top five plastic polluters, 50% on Forest 500 and the EU Taxonomy, Tier 4 on BBFAW, and 50 on the human rights benchmark.
      • 
          
    • Coverage varies. CDP, the two exit lists, the human rights benchmark, EU competition fines, Women on Boards and the emissions trend could only be part-checked, because full lists need a sign-in, weren’t readable or don’t compare fairly. Those rules count only what was confirmed.
      • 
          
    • EU point (c) on UN Global Compact violators uses Norway’s published findings as a stand-in, because those verdicts are sold by data vendors.
      • 
          
    • Funds are checked on what their index rules say. A blank means we could not confirm it.
